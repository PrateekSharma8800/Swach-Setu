Why it matters

Small Actions, Big Environmental Impact

How we handle waste at home decides whether it becomes a resource or a pollutant.

Waste Segregation

Separating waste at home into wet, dry and hazardous keeps recyclable materials clean and usable. Mixed waste is hard to recover and usually ends up in landfills.

  • Keeps recyclables free from food contamination
  • Lets wet waste be turned into compost
  • Required at source under India's Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016

Recycling

Recycling turns used paper, plastic, glass and metal into new products. It reduces the need to mine, cut trees or drill for new raw materials.

  • Conserves natural resources like trees, water and minerals
  • Uses less energy than making products from scratch
  • Creates livelihoods in the recycling sector

Responsible Disposal

Items like batteries, medicines and electronics contain chemicals and heavy metals. Thrown in the general bin, they can leak into soil and groundwater.

  • Prevents toxic substances from polluting soil and water
  • Protects sanitation workers from injury and exposure
  • Recovers valuable metals from old electronics
